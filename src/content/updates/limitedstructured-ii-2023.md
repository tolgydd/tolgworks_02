Designed as an introductory exercise for first-year architecture students within the Basic Design Studio, *LimitedStructed-II* investigates the relationship between materiality, structural behaviour, and form-making through experimental model construction. The workshop aims to cultivate an awareness of structural principles and load-bearing capacities, encouraging students to explore how physical properties, material constraints, and construction techniques inform architectural design.

Working within a deliberately restricted material palette, participants are allowed a maximum of three materials: either two structural elements and one binding material, or one structural element and two binding materials. The use of adhesives is strictly prohibited, requiring all components to be assembled through alternative joining and connection techniques. Within these constraints, students are challenged to conceive and construct a self-supporting structure reaching a minimum height of 15 cm.

The exercise places particular emphasis on the relationship between structural configuration and material behaviour, inviting students to investigate principles of balance, tension, compression, load distribution, and equilibrium through direct physical experimentation. Rather than relying on predefined structural solutions, participants are encouraged to discover and develop their own construction logics through iterative making, testing, and adjustment.

The workshop culminates in a series of load-bearing experiments in which the completed structures are subjected to progressively different stone weights. This final testing phase reveals the structural capacities and limitations of each proposal, transforming abstract notions of stability and strength into tangible, observable experiences.

Ultimately, *LimitedStructed-II* approaches model-making not merely as a representational practice, but as a means of thinking through construction. By working with limited resources and explicit structural constraints, students are introduced to the fundamental relationship between material, connection, geometry, and load, discovering how architectural form can emerge from the negotiation between structural necessity and design exploration.
