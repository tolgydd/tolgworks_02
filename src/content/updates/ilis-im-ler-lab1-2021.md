*"Light is everywhere. In fact, it is light that makes everywhere possible in the first place. A photon bounces off a surface, catches our eye, and establishes our relationship with that surface. What disappears in one instant is brought into existence in the next. Light brings us into contact with the space we inhabit.*

*Another participant in this encounter is sound. When it comes to the relationship between our bodies and the space they occupy, sound comes to the rescue where light reaches its limits. Within this acoustic and luminous framework, the presence of our bodies is equally unique: an existence specific to that particular moment, unrepeatable and singular.*

*But what if we were to detach light from material space as much as possible? What if we were to wrap ourselves in it, surround ourselves with it? What if sound, instead of giving us answers, began asking questions? And what if, rather than remaining still and recording these encounters, our bodies were to respond to those questions?*

*These are precisely the questions this workshop sets out to explore. The encounter between light and sound. An encounter, a meeting, a performance...*

*- Then, dance!!*  
*- Colour!!"*



&nbsp;

The project explores spatial reconfiguration through the interplay of material and immaterial environments, bringing together projection mapping, soundscape, and the concept of performative space. Situated at the intersection of physical and digital layers, the research investigates how sound, light, smoke, and other immaterial elements can define, transform, and temporarily delimit space.

The workshop extends this exploration beyond visual perception, examining how participants experience and interact with environments shaped by these ephemeral conditions. Through a series of performative experiments, space is approached not as a fixed architectural container, but as an evolving construct produced through sensory encounters, bodily presence, and movement.

Conceived as an experimental research laboratory, the project investigates the possibility of art defining a space that can be inhabited rather than merely observed: an environment to be experienced from within, rather than an object positioned before, beside, or behind its audience.
