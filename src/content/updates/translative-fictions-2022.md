> *"I speak, I speak," says Marco, "but those who listen hear only what they wish to hear. (...) It is not the voice that gives direction to a narrative, but the ear."*
>
> ***Invisible Cities, Italo Calvino***
>
> Space exists, whether we are part of it or not. Yet, the moment we enter it, our senses redefine it for us. Our stories, our senses, and the image of space enter an endless process of mutual transformation.
>
> In this context, the workshop focuses on the perception of the user as a subject within space, exploring how sound is heard, perceived, interpreted, and reproduced through different means of representation. The materials generated throughout this process sometimes become outcomes in their own right, and sometimes serve as starting points for entirely new processes of production.



Taking soundscape as its conceptual point of departure, the workshop approaches the built environment as an accumulation of acoustic impressions, spatial associations, and subjective experiences. Participants begin by exploring the university campus through sound, collecting audio recordings within a defined period and assembling them into individual sound collages. These recordings are subsequently mixed, redistributed randomly, and detached from their original contexts, inviting participants to encounter unfamiliar soundscapes without knowing where or how they were produced.

Each participant is then asked to translate their assigned sound collage into a visual representation, interpreting its rhythms, intensities, textures, distances, and spatial associations through drawing. The resulting drawings are once again mixed and randomly redistributed, this time becoming the starting point for a new act of interpretation: participants are invited to imagine and write the narratives they perceive within these visual compositions.

Structured as a chain of translations, the process deliberately separates each stage of production from its point of origin. Much like a game of telephone, information is continuously transformed as it moves between auditory, visual, and textual forms, with each participant introducing their own associations, interpretations, and imaginative contributions. No participant has access to the original context or narrative behind the material they receive.

At the end of the workshop, the sound recordings, drawings, and written narratives are brought together, allowing the successive transformations of each initial soundscape to be traced and compared. The resulting compositions reveal how a single spatial experience can generate multiple, sometimes entirely unexpected narratives as it passes through different perceptual and representational frameworks.

Rather than seeking an accurate or objective translation of space, the workshop investigates the productive ambiguity between perception and representation. It questions how architectural space is understood beyond its physical and visual dimensions, and how its acoustic qualities can trigger new spatial imaginaries. Through this process, sound becomes not merely an attribute of the built environment, but a generative medium through which alternative readings, representations, and narratives of space can emerge.
