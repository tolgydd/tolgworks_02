> "I exist, I am here. Others exist too, and so do other things. I am neither this nor that; I find myself within a space (or perhaps not) that is neither this nor that. And as I said, there are others. They touch me, and I, in turn, take part in this act of touching. I exist, and when they touch me, I seem inclined to become someone else, another version of myself. Even before they touched me, I was never entirely this or that. And through this reciprocal act, neither are we any longer entirely this or that. This is our becoming another kind of us, transforming without ever settling, together with the space (or perhaps not) we inhabit.
>
> Now my hands and arms are here, or perhaps not. What is real is no longer quite real, or perhaps entirely new realities have come into being. This is a search, an interrogation of reality, a cloud of questions that no longer cares about answers."



The workshop investigates the relationship between material and immaterial space, questioning how architectural reality can be altered through elements that exist beyond conventional physical boundaries. Light, sound, and fluid or ephemeral materials become instruments of spatial intervention, offering possibilities for transforming the perception and experience of an existing environment. Rather than approaching space as a fixed and neutral container, the workshop considers it as a condition that can be continuously reconstructed through sensory perception, interaction, and collective intervention.

The conceptual framework draws on the notion of immaterial architecture, exploring forms of spatial existence that emerge through perception, imagination, and atmospheric conditions rather than solely through physical construction. The distinction between the sensible and the intelligible, alongside the relationship between the virtual and the actual, provides a basis for investigating how spaces can be experienced beyond their immediately visible and tangible properties. References such as Diller + Scofidio's *Blur Building* illustrate the potential of ephemeral elements, particularly mist and atmospheric conditions, to challenge conventional architectural boundaries and produce alternative spatial experiences.

Soundscape introduces an additional dimension to this exploration. Drawing on R. Murray Schafer's understanding of the acoustic environment, sound is approached as a means of perceiving and interpreting surroundings through auditory and sensory relationships. Qualities such as intensity, distance, directionality, repetition, reverberation, and the distinction between foreground and background contribute to the mental construction of spatial landscapes. The workshop also considers the potential of translating acoustic information into architectural and visual forms, exploring how sound can become a generative medium for spatial representation and production.

Projection mapping further extends this investigation by introducing digital imagery onto physical surfaces. Through surface-specific projections, architectural elements become active participants in the construction of alternative spatial narratives, allowing the boundaries between the material environment and its digital counterpart to become increasingly ambiguous.

At the intersection of these approaches, İliş-im-ler.3 seeks to question how neutrality can be identified, disrupted, and reconstructed. By employing seemingly neutral elements and immaterial interventions, the workshop proposes a collective search for a new spatial condition: one that is neither entirely physical nor virtual, neither wholly tangible nor intangible. Ultimately, it approaches architectural production as an open-ended process of experimentation, in which space is not simply occupied or observed, but continuously reimagined and brought into being through the relationships established between bodies, materials, perception, and environment.
