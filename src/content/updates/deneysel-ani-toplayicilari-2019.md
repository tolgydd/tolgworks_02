An experimental workshop exploring the relationship between urban space, collective memory, and narrative representation. Taking the historic urban fabric of Mardin as its point of departure, the workshop approached the city not as a fixed geographical entity but as a cumulative and permeable field of spatial traces, memories, associations, and individual perceptions.

Through psychogeographic exploration, architectural mapping, and experimental representational techniques, participants investigated ways of collecting, extracting, manipulating, and recombining fragments of the urban environment. Drawings, images, objects, spatial observations, and personal associations became material for constructing composite maps and alternative spatial narratives.

Rather than documenting the city as it exists, the process sought to construct new relationships between its fragments, allowing individual recollections and collective imaginaries to intersect. Mapping thus became not merely a tool for reading the city, but an active process of interpretation, storytelling, and spatial production.
