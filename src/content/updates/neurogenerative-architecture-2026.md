Neuroarchitecture has become good at measuring how built environments affect us, and generative design at producing design alternatives through algorithms. Yet the two rarely meet: the brain and body data that neuroarchitecture collects seldom become direct inputs to design. This paper brings the two together under the term *neurogenerative architecture*.

[[image 1]]

Drawing on a comparative review of research on brain-computer interfaces (BCI), EEG-based, neuroadaptive and neuroresponsive design, and generative design, the paper sets out a conceptual model in which neurophysiological data are collected, processed into indicators of a person's state, translated into design parameters and given spatial form by generative systems in hybrid environments, shaping light, sound, audiovisual atmosphere and spatial behaviour. The result is a continuous neurofeedback loop between people and space, where space is no longer a fixed object but a dynamic system that evolves together with its users.

Uçkan, B., Atukeren, A., Özmutlu, B., Dağ, A., Aydoğdu, T., Pektetik, U., Gün, A., Gürer, E., & Karakaş, T. Paper presented at MSTAS 2026, XX. Symposium on Digital Design in Architecture, TED University, Ankara, 21–23 June 2026.
