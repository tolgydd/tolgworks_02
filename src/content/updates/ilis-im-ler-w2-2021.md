*"Light travels at 299,792,458 metres per second. It emerges from a source and sets off on its relentless journey, at a speed beyond the eye to see or the mind to grasp.*

*Sometimes, there is nothing in its way, and it claims all the darkness it encounters, covers it, transforms it. Sometimes, to pass through certain obstacles, it must change, transform itself. Its colour shifts, or it strikes an obstacle and becomes part of another journey. Its destination changes.*

*And sometimes, the obstacle is stubborn. It grabs light by the arm and refuses to let go.*

*At this unplanned stop, light begins to tell us its story, in a hurry. Sometimes, it brings the very obstacle itself into the story. Sometimes, it is joined by other companions, those who set out alongside it from the very same source.*

*And now, we invite you to lend an ear to the light, to listen to its stories, to become guests in the spaces within them.*

*At this stop, where the physical meets the digital, the visible intersects with the audible, and that which remains forever meets that which disappears, we invite you to set off in pursuit of entirely new stories."*



&nbsp;

The project explores the spatial reconfiguration of material and immaterial environments through projection mapping, soundscape, and the creative reuse of textiles. Throughout the workshop, a multidimensional narrative was developed and translated onto surfaces of varying textures and material properties, experimenting with projection, layering, interweaving, and fragmentation as means of spatial production.

Situated at the intersection of physical and digital realms, the process sought to investigate new spatial possibilities emerging from the interaction between projected imagery, tangible materials, and their material and immaterial qualities. The exploration extended beyond the visual through the integration of soundscape, introducing music composed during the workshop as an active spatial component rather than a mere accompaniment. Through this interplay of image, sound, light, and recycled materials, the workshop experimented with a spatial model in which the environment is continuously constructed, transformed, and experienced through the convergence of multiple sensory and material layers.
